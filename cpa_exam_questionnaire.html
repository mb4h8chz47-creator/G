CPA Exam Practice Questionnaire — 200 Questions

Sections covered: FAR (Q1–70) | AUD (Q71–130) | REG (Q131–170) | BAR (Q171–200)

PART 1: Financial Accounting and Reporting (FAR) — Questions 1–70

Q1. Under U.S. GAAP, which inventory cost-flow assumption is not permitted?

A) FIFO
B) LIFO
C) Weighted-average
D) Replacement cost

Answer: D) Replacement cost

Explanation: GAAP permits FIFO, LIFO, and weighted-average (as well as specific identification). Replacement cost is not a permitted cost-flow assumption; it is a market value concept used in lower-of-cost-or-market analysis, not an inventory costing method.

Q2. A company using LIFO reports ending inventory of $40,000 and a LIFO reserve of $12,000. What would ending inventory be under FIFO?

A) $28,000
B) $40,000
C) $52,000
D) $48,000

Answer: C) $52,000

Explanation: FIFO inventory = LIFO inventory + LIFO reserve = $40,000 + $12,000 = $52,000. The LIFO reserve represents the cumulative difference between FIFO and LIFO inventory values.

Q3. Which of the following is classified as a financing activity on the statement of cash flows?

A) Purchase of equipment
B) Payment of dividends
C) Collection of accounts receivable
D) Payment of income taxes

Answer: B) Payment of dividends

Explanation: Financing activities include transactions with owners (issuing/repurchasing stock, paying dividends) and creditors (borrowing/repaying debt). Equipment purchases are investing; AR collections and tax payments are operating.

Q4. Under the allowance method, when a previously written-off account is recovered, the first entry is to:

A) Debit Cash, Credit Bad Debt Expense
B) Debit Accounts Receivable, Credit Allowance for Doubtful Accounts
C) Debit Allowance for Doubtful Accounts, Credit Accounts Receivable
D) Debit Cash, Credit Accounts Receivable

Answer: B) Debit Accounts Receivable, Credit Allowance for Doubtful Accounts

Explanation: Recovery requires two entries: (1) reverse the write-off by debiting AR and crediting Allowance, then (2) record the cash collection by debiting Cash and crediting AR. This restores the account before recording receipt.

Q5. A bond is issued at a premium when:

A) The stated rate equals the market rate
B) The stated rate is less than the market rate
C) The stated rate is greater than the market rate
D) The bond is callable

Answer: C) The stated rate is greater than the market rate

Explanation: When a bond's coupon rate exceeds the prevailing market (yield) rate, investors will pay more than face value — a premium — to obtain the higher interest payments. The premium is amortized over the bond's life, reducing interest expense each period.

Q6. Goodwill is:

A) Amortized over its useful life, not to exceed 40 years
B) Tested for impairment annually and not amortized (public companies)
C) Always amortized over 15 years for GAAP purposes
D) Expensed immediately upon acquisition

Answer: B) Tested for impairment annually and not amortized (public companies)

Explanation: Under ASC 350, goodwill from a business combination is not amortized for public companies. Instead it is tested for impairment at least annually (or when triggering events occur). Private companies may elect the accounting alternative to amortize goodwill over up to 10 years.

Q7. Which depreciation method results in the highest depreciation expense in the first year of an asset's life?

A) Straight-line
B) Units of production
C) Double-declining balance
D) Sum-of-the-years'-digits

Answer: C) Double-declining balance

Explanation: Double-declining balance (DDB) applies twice the straight-line rate to the beginning book value, producing the largest first-year expense. Sum-of-years'-digits is also accelerated but generally lower than DDB in year one. Straight-line spreads cost evenly; units of production depends on actual usage.

Q8. The direct method of preparing the operating section of the statement of cash flows:

A) Adjusts net income for non-cash items
B) Shows actual cash inflows and outflows from operations
C) Is required by FASB
D) Is the most commonly used method in practice

Answer: B) Shows actual cash inflows and outflows from operations

Explanation: The direct method presents major classes of gross cash receipts and payments (e.g., cash received from customers, cash paid to suppliers). FASB encourages but does not require it. The indirect method — which starts with net income and makes adjustments — is far more common in practice.

Q9. Under GAAP, research and development costs are generally:

A) Capitalized and amortized over the estimated useful life
B) Expensed as incurred
C) Capitalized if the project is commercially viable
D) Deferred until revenue is recognized

Answer: B) Expensed as incurred

Explanation: ASC 730 requires R&D costs to be expensed as incurred due to the high uncertainty of future benefits. Exception: costs related to software development after technological feasibility is established may be capitalized.

Q10. A contingent liability should be accrued when:

A) It is possible and the amount can be estimated
B) It is probable and the amount can be reasonably estimated
C) It is reasonably possible and estimable
D) It is remote but material

Answer: B) It is probable and the amount can be reasonably estimated

Explanation: Under ASC 450, a loss contingency is accrued only when (1) it is probable that a liability has been incurred, and (2) the amount can be reasonably estimated. If only possible (not probable) or not estimable, disclosure — not accrual — is required.

Q11. Which of the following would increase a company's current ratio?

A) Purchasing inventory on account
B) Collecting accounts receivable
C) Paying a current liability with cash
D) Declaring a cash dividend

Answer: C) Paying a current liability with cash

Explanation: If the current ratio is above 1.0 (which is typical), paying a current liability with cash increases the ratio because both numerator (current assets) and denominator (current liabilities) decrease, but the proportion improves. Buying inventory on account and collecting AR leave the ratio unchanged; declaring a dividend creates a current liability.

Q12. Operating leases under ASC 842 require lessees to recognize:

A) Only rent expense; no balance sheet impact
B) A right-of-use asset and a lease liability
C) A right-of-use asset only
D) A lease liability only

Answer: B) A right-of-use asset and a lease liability

Explanation: ASC 842 (effective for public companies 2019) eliminated most off-balance-sheet leases. Both operating and finance leases now require a right-of-use (ROU) asset and a corresponding lease liability on the balance sheet. The income statement treatment differs: operating leases show a single straight-line lease expense.

Q13. Under the percentage-of-completion method, revenue is recognized:

A) When the project is completed
B) Proportionally as work progresses
C) When cash is collected
D) At the point of delivery

Answer: B) Proportionally as work progresses

Explanation: Percentage-of-completion (now incorporated into ASC 606's "over time" recognition) recognizes revenue as performance obligations are satisfied over time, based on progress metrics (e.g., costs incurred / total estimated costs). This reflects the continuous transfer of control to the customer.

Q14. The equity method of accounting is used when an investor has:

A) Less than 20% ownership with no significant influence
B) 20%–50% ownership, indicating significant influence
C) More than 50% ownership
D) 100% ownership of a subsidiary

Answer: B) 20%–50% ownership, indicating significant influence

Explanation: The equity method is presumed appropriate when ownership is 20%–50%, indicating significant influence over operating and financial decisions. Below 20% generally uses the fair value method; above 50% typically requires consolidation.

Q15. Treasury stock is reported on the balance sheet as:

A) An asset
B) A contra-equity account
C) A liability
D) Additional paid-in capital

Answer: B) A contra-equity account

Explanation: Treasury stock represents shares repurchased by the company and not yet retired. It is recorded at cost and presented as a deduction from total stockholders' equity — a contra-equity account — not as an asset, since a company cannot own itself.

Q16. Which financial statement is also known as the statement of financial position?

A) Income statement
B) Statement of cash flows
C) Balance sheet
D) Statement of stockholders' equity

Answer: C) Balance sheet

Explanation: The balance sheet (statement of financial position) presents a company's assets, liabilities, and equity at a specific point in time, following the accounting equation: Assets = Liabilities + Equity.

Q17. A company sells a product with a 2-year warranty. The estimated warranty cost is $500 per unit sold. In Year 1, the company sells 1,000 units. What is the warranty expense recognized in Year 1?

A) $0
B) $250,000
C) $500,000
D) Amount of actual warranty claims in Year 1

Answer: C) $500,000

Explanation: Under GAAP's matching principle, warranty expense is accrued in the period of sale, not when claims are made. Total estimated expense = 1,000 units × $500 = $500,000 is recognized in Year 1 regardless of when actual repairs occur.

Q18. Comprehensive income includes:

A) Net income only
B) Net income plus other comprehensive income (OCI) items
C) Revenue minus expenses
D) Operating income plus non-operating items

Answer: B) Net income plus other comprehensive income (OCI) items

Explanation: Comprehensive income = Net income + Other Comprehensive Income. OCI items include unrealized gains/losses on available-for-sale securities, foreign currency translation adjustments, pension liability adjustments, and effective portion of cash flow hedges.

Q19. Under GAAP, a company receives $120,000 on January 1 for a 12-month service contract. How much revenue is recognized in the first quarter?

A) $120,000
B) $0
C) $30,000
D) $10,000

Answer: C) $30,000

Explanation: Revenue is recognized as the performance obligation (services) is satisfied over time. $120,000 ÷ 12 months × 3 months = $30,000 per quarter. The remaining $90,000 is recorded as deferred revenue (liability) on the balance sheet.

Q20. Which method of computing depreciation is based on actual usage of an asset?

A) Straight-line
B) Double-declining balance
C) Units of production
D) Sum-of-the-years'-digits

Answer: C) Units of production

Explanation: Units of production depreciation allocates cost based on actual output or usage (e.g., miles driven, units manufactured). It is appropriate when an asset's usefulness is more closely tied to usage than to time passage.

Q21. Preferred stock that must be redeemed by the issuer at a fixed date is classified as:

A) Equity
B) Liability
C) Mezzanine equity
D) Retained earnings

Answer: B) Liability

Explanation: Mandatorily redeemable preferred stock requires the issuer to repurchase shares on a specified date, creating an obligation similar to debt. Under ASC 480, such instruments are classified as liabilities, not equity.

Q22. Under GAAP, which costs are capitalized as part of inventory?

A) Selling costs
B) General and administrative costs
C) Manufacturing overhead
D) Interest on borrowed funds

Answer: C) Manufacturing overhead

Explanation: Inventory costs include direct materials, direct labor, and manufacturing overhead (both variable and fixed). Selling, general, and administrative costs are period costs expensed as incurred. Interest is generally not capitalized into inventory.

Q23. A patent is purchased for $500,000 with a remaining legal life of 15 years and an estimated useful life of 10 years. Annual amortization under GAAP is:

A) $33,333
B) $50,000
C) $25,000
D) $0 — patents are not amortized

Answer: B) $50,000

Explanation: Intangible assets with finite useful lives are amortized over the shorter of their legal life or useful life. Here, the useful life (10 years) is shorter. $500,000 ÷ 10 = $50,000 per year.

Q24. Under the cost method of accounting for investments, dividends received are recorded as:

A) A reduction in the investment account
B) Dividend income
C) A return of capital
D) Unrealized gain

Answer: B) Dividend income

Explanation: Under the fair value / cost method (used for investments with less than 20% ownership and no significant influence), dividends received are recorded as dividend income. Under the equity method, dividends reduce the investment account.

Q25. Which of the following is an example of a subsequent event that requires adjustment of the financial statements?

A) A major natural disaster that occurred after year-end
B) Settlement of a lawsuit at an amount different from the recorded estimate
C) Issuance of bonds after year-end
D) A business combination completed after year-end

Answer: B) Settlement of a lawsuit at an amount different from the recorded estimate

Explanation: Type I subsequent events (recognized) provide additional evidence about conditions existing at the balance sheet date and require adjustment. A lawsuit settlement reveals information about the pre-existing liability. Type II events (unrecognized) — like disasters or new financings — are disclosed but don't adjust the statements.

Q26. The quick ratio excludes which of the following from current assets?

A) Cash
B) Marketable securities
C) Inventory
D) Accounts receivable

Answer: C) Inventory

Explanation: Quick ratio = (Cash + Short-term investments + Accounts receivable) ÷ Current liabilities. Inventory is excluded because it is the least liquid current asset and may not convert to cash quickly. Prepaid expenses are also excluded.

Q27. A change from FIFO to LIFO is accounted for as:

A) A change in accounting estimate — prospective treatment
B) A change in accounting principle — retrospective treatment
C) A change in accounting principle — prospective treatment (because retrospective is impracticable)
D) A correction of an error

Answer: C) A change in accounting principle — prospective treatment (because retrospective is impracticable)

Explanation: Switching from FIFO to LIFO is a change in accounting principle. Normally, changes in principle require retrospective treatment. However, LIFO is unique — it is generally impracticable to apply retrospectively, so only the new LIFO beginning balance is established and prospective treatment is used.

Q28. Earnings per share (EPS) is required to be reported on the face of the income statement by:

A) All entities
B) Public companies only
C) Companies with more than $10 million in assets
D) Companies with more than 500 shareholders

Answer: B) Public companies only

Explanation: ASC 260 requires EPS disclosure only for public entities (those with publicly traded common stock or potential common stock). Private companies are not required to present EPS, though they may elect to do so.

Q29. The fair value option under ASC 825 allows companies to elect fair value measurement for:

A) All assets and liabilities
B) Eligible financial instruments, on an instrument-by-instrument basis
C) Only trading securities
D) Only derivatives

Answer: B) Eligible financial instruments, on an instrument-by-instrument basis

Explanation: The fair value option (FVO) permits — but does not require — entities to measure eligible financial assets and liabilities at fair value. The election is made instrument by instrument at initial recognition, with subsequent changes in fair value reported in earnings.

Q30. Which level of the fair value hierarchy uses unobservable inputs?

A) Level 1
B) Level 2
C) Level 3
D) Level 4

Answer: C) Level 3

Explanation: ASC 820 defines three levels: Level 1 = quoted prices in active markets; Level 2 = observable inputs other than Level 1 quotes (e.g., comparable transactions); Level 3 = unobservable inputs based on the entity's own assumptions. Level 3 measurements involve the most subjectivity and uncertainty.

Q31. A deferred tax liability arises when:

A) Book income is less than taxable income
B) Book income exceeds taxable income (temporary difference)
C) A tax loss carryforward exists
D) The statutory tax rate decreases

Answer: B) Book income exceeds taxable income (temporary difference)

Explanation: A deferred tax liability (DTL) occurs when taxable income will be higher in future periods than book income — i.e., taxes owed later. Common examples include using accelerated depreciation for tax and straight-line for books. The entity has recognized income now for GAAP but will pay more taxes later.

Q32. Under ASC 606 (Revenue Recognition), the five-step model begins with:

A) Determine the transaction price
B) Identify the contract with a customer
C) Identify performance obligations
D) Allocate the transaction price

Answer: B) Identify the contract with a customer

Explanation: The five steps are: (1) Identify the contract, (2) Identify performance obligations, (3) Determine the transaction price, (4) Allocate the transaction price to performance obligations, (5) Recognize revenue when/as each obligation is satisfied.

Q33. A company has a defined benefit pension plan. The service cost component of net pension expense is reported in:

A) Other comprehensive income
B) Operating expenses
C) Other income and expense
D) Retained earnings

Answer: B) Operating expenses

Explanation: Under ASC 715 (amended), the service cost component is reported with other employee compensation costs (operating expenses). All other components (interest cost, expected return on assets, amortization of prior service cost, actuarial gains/losses) are reported outside of operating income, often in other income/expense.

Q34. Foreign currency transaction gains and losses are reported:

A) In other comprehensive income
B) In net income in the period they occur
C) As a direct adjustment to retained earnings
D) As deferred items until the transaction settles

Answer: B) In net income in the period they occur

Explanation: Transaction gains and losses (from monetary items denominated in foreign currencies) are recognized immediately in net income as exchange rates fluctuate. In contrast, translation adjustments (converting foreign subsidiary statements) are reported in OCI as part of cumulative translation adjustment (CTA).

Q35. Under GAAP, capitalized interest costs are included in:

A) Interest expense on the income statement
B) The cost of the qualifying asset being constructed
C) Paid-in capital
D) Deferred charges

Answer: B) The cost of the qualifying asset being constructed

Explanation: ASC 835-20 requires capitalization of interest costs incurred during the construction of a qualifying asset (one that requires time to prepare for use). The capitalized interest increases the asset's cost base and is subsequently depreciated over the asset's useful life.

Q36. On a classified balance sheet, which of the following is a long-term liability?

A) Accounts payable
B) Current portion of long-term debt
C) Bonds payable due in 5 years
D) Accrued wages

Answer: C) Bonds payable due in 5 years

Explanation: Long-term liabilities are obligations not due within one year (or the operating cycle). Bonds payable maturing in 5 years are long-term. Accounts payable, accrued wages, and the current portion of long-term debt are all current liabilities.

Q37. Which of the following transactions would not appear on a statement of cash flows?

A) Depreciation expense
B) Conversion of bonds payable to common stock
C) Cash purchase of equipment
D) Payment of dividends

Answer: B) Conversion of bonds payable to common stock

Explanation: Non-cash investing and financing activities (like converting debt to equity, acquiring assets through capital leases, or exchanging property) do not affect cash and therefore are not reported in the cash flow statement body. They are disclosed in a supplemental schedule. However, depreciation appears as an add-back under the indirect method.

Q38. A company declares a 3-for-1 stock split. Before the split, 100,000 shares were outstanding at a par value of $3 per share. After the split:

A) 300,000 shares outstanding; $1 par value
B) 300,000 shares outstanding; $3 par value
C) 100,000 shares outstanding; $1 par value
D) 33,333 shares outstanding; $9 par value

Answer: A) 300,000 shares outstanding; $1 par value

Explanation: A stock split increases shares outstanding proportionally and reduces par value proportionally. Total par value (equity) remains unchanged. 100,000 × 3 = 300,000 shares; $3 ÷ 3 = $1 par value. Total par value = 300,000 × $1 = $300,000 = same as before.

Q39. Under the indirect method, an increase in accounts receivable is shown as:

A) An addition to net income
B) A deduction from net income
C) An investing activity
D) A financing activity

Answer: B) A deduction from net income

Explanation: An increase in AR means revenue was recognized (included in net income) but cash was not yet received. To reconcile net income to cash from operations, the AR increase is subtracted. Decreases in AR are added back.

Q40. The lower of cost or net realizable value (LCNRV) rule applies to which inventory cost-flow methods under current GAAP?

A) LIFO and retail only
B) FIFO and weighted-average only
C) All inventory methods except LIFO and retail
D) All inventory methods, including LIFO

Answer: C) All inventory methods except LIFO and retail

Explanation: ASU 2015-11 replaced "lower of cost or market" with "lower of cost or net realizable value" (LCNRV) for entities using FIFO or weighted-average. Entities using LIFO or the retail inventory method still use the older lower-of-cost-or-market approach with its ceiling and floor.

Q41. A variable interest entity (VIE) is consolidated by the entity that:

A) Holds the majority of voting rights
B) Is the primary beneficiary — absorbs most losses or receives most benefits
C) Provides the most debt financing
D) Has the oldest relationship with the VIE

Answer: B) Is the primary beneficiary — absorbs most losses or receives most benefits

Explanation: ASC 810 requires VIEs (entities lacking sufficient equity to finance their own activities) to be consolidated by the primary beneficiary — the entity that (1) has the power to direct the activities that most significantly affect the VIE's economic performance, and (2) has the obligation to absorb losses or receive benefits that could be significant.

Q42. Which of the following is not an element of the conceptual framework's qualitative characteristics?

A) Relevance
B) Faithful representation
C) Comparability
D) Liquidity

Answer: D) Liquidity

Explanation: The FASB Conceptual Framework identifies two fundamental qualities (relevance, faithful representation) and four enhancing qualities (comparability, verifiability, timeliness, understandability). Liquidity is a financial characteristic of an entity, not a qualitative characteristic of financial information.

Q43. When a company issues a stock dividend of less than 20–25%, the entry is recorded at:

A) Par value
B) Fair market value
C) Book value
D) No entry required

Answer: B) Fair market value

Explanation: Small stock dividends (less than 20–25% of outstanding shares) are recorded at the fair market value of the shares issued, transferring amounts from retained earnings to paid-in capital. Large stock dividends (≥20–25%) are recorded at par value.

Q44. Under ASC 350, an intangible asset with an indefinite useful life is:

A) Amortized over 40 years
B) Tested for impairment at least annually
C) Expensed immediately
D) Amortized over the asset's legal life

Answer: B) Tested for impairment at least annually

Explanation: Indefinite-lived intangibles (like certain trademarks or licenses) are not amortized. Instead, they are tested for impairment at least annually or whenever events indicate potential impairment. Finite-lived intangibles are amortized over their useful lives.

Q45. A capital lease (finance lease under ASC 842) is recognized by the lessee when:

A) The lease term is 75% or more of the asset's remaining economic life
B) The present value of lease payments is 90% or more of fair value
C) Either A or B (or other qualifying criteria)
D) The lessee has an option to purchase at fair market value

Answer: C) Either A or B (or other qualifying criteria)

Explanation: ASC 842 classifies a lease as a finance lease if any one of five criteria is met: (1) ownership transfer, (2) purchase option likely to be exercised, (3) lease term ≥ major part of economic life (generally 75%), (4) PV of payments ≥ substantially all of fair value (generally 90%), or (5) specialized asset with no alternative use.

Q46. The going concern assumption means that:

A) The entity will be liquidated within the next year
B) The entity will continue operating indefinitely into the future
C) Assets are recorded at liquidation value
D) The financial statements must be prepared on a cash basis

Answer: B) The entity will continue operating indefinitely into the future

Explanation: The going concern (continuity) assumption underlies financial reporting. It assumes the business will remain in operation long enough to fulfill its objectives and commitments. This justifies recording assets at historical cost rather than liquidation value. ASC 205-40 requires disclosure when substantial doubt about going concern exists.

Q47. A company exchanges a machine with a book value of $20,000 and a fair value of $35,000 for another machine with a fair value of $35,000. The exchange has commercial substance. What is the gain recognized?

A) $0
B) $15,000
C) $35,000
D) $20,000

Answer: B) $15,000

Explanation: When an exchange has commercial substance, the asset given up is recorded at its fair value, and any gain or loss is recognized immediately. Gain = FV of asset given up − Book value = $35,000 − $20,000 = $15,000.

Q48. Which of the following costs is not capitalized as part of property, plant, and equipment?

A) Purchase price
B) Sales taxes on purchase
C) Training costs for employees to use the equipment
D) Freight and installation costs

Answer: C) Training costs for employees to use the equipment

Explanation: Capitalized PP&E costs include purchase price, sales taxes, freight, installation, and testing — all costs necessary to bring the asset to its intended use. Training costs are expensed as incurred because they benefit employees, not the asset itself.

Q49. Net realizable value (NRV) of accounts receivable equals:

A) Gross AR only
B) Gross AR minus allowance for doubtful accounts
C) Gross AR plus interest receivable
D) Gross AR minus sales discounts taken

Answer: B) Gross AR minus allowance for doubtful accounts

Explanation: NRV of AR = Gross accounts receivable − Allowance for doubtful accounts. This represents the amount the company expects to actually collect. It is the amount reported on the balance sheet.

Q50. Under GAAP, which of the following is reported as an operating activity in the statement of cash flows?

A) Proceeds from issuing long-term debt
B) Interest paid
C) Dividends paid
D) Purchase of investments

Answer: B) Interest paid

Explanation: Under U.S. GAAP, interest paid is classified as an operating activity (unlike IFRS, which allows it to be financing). Proceeds from debt issuance = financing; dividends paid = financing; purchase of investments = investing.

Q51. A company has the following data: Total assets = $500,000; Total liabilities = $200,000; Common stock = $150,000. What is retained earnings?

A) $300,000
B) $150,000
C) $350,000
D) $50,000

Answer: B) $150,000

Explanation: Equity = Assets − Liabilities = $500,000 − $200,000 = $300,000. Retained Earnings = Total Equity − Common Stock = $300,000 − $150,000 = $150,000.

Q52. Discontinued operations are presented:

A) Within continuing operations, net of tax
B) As a separate line item below continuing operations, net of tax
C) As part of other comprehensive income
D) Only in the notes to financial statements

Answer: B) As a separate line item below continuing operations, net of tax

Explanation: ASC 205-20 requires discontinued operations — representing a strategic shift that has a major effect on operations — to be presented separately in the income statement below income from continuing operations, shown net of tax.

Q53. Under the modified accrual basis used by governmental funds, revenues are recognized when:

A) Earned and measurable
B) Received in cash
C) Available and measurable
D) Approved by the legislature

Answer: C) Available and measurable

Explanation: Governmental fund revenue recognition requires revenues to be (1) measurable and (2) available (collectible within the current period or soon enough to pay current obligations, generally within 60 days of year-end). This differs from full accrual where "earned" is the key criterion.

Q54. The financial statements of a state government include all of the following except:

A) Government-wide statements (accrual basis)
B) Fund financial statements (modified accrual for governmental funds)
C) Segment reporting by business line
D) Management's Discussion and Analysis (MD&A)

Answer: C) Segment reporting by business line

Explanation: GASB-required governmental financial reporting includes government-wide statements, fund statements, notes, RSI (including MD&A and budgetary comparison schedules), and other required supplementary information. Segment reporting as defined under FASB standards does not apply to governments.

Q55. Which of the following is a proprietary fund type used in governmental accounting?

A) General Fund
B) Debt Service Fund
C) Enterprise Fund
D) Special Revenue Fund

Answer: C) Enterprise Fund

Explanation: Proprietary funds (business-type activities) include Enterprise Funds (activities funded primarily through user charges, like utilities) and Internal Service Funds (services provided to other government departments on a cost-reimbursement basis). General, Debt Service, and Special Revenue are governmental funds.

Q56. An entity reports basic EPS of $3.00 and has 100,000 dilutive stock options outstanding with an exercise price of $10 (market price = $20). What is the incremental shares denominator addition using the treasury stock method?

A) 100,000 shares
B) 50,000 shares
C) 25,000 shares
D) 0 shares

Answer: B) 50,000 shares

Explanation: Treasury stock method: Proceeds from exercise = 100,000 × $10 = $1,000,000. Shares repurchased with proceeds = $1,000,000 ÷ $20 = 50,000. Net incremental shares = 100,000 − 50,000 = 50,000 shares added to the diluted EPS denominator.

Q57. Under IFRS, inventory is written down to:

A) Lower of cost or net realizable value, with reversals permitted
B) Lower of cost or market, with no reversals
C) Replacement cost
D) Lower of cost or fair value

Answer: A) Lower of cost or net realizable value, with reversals permitted

Explanation: IFRS (IAS 2) requires inventory at the lower of cost or NRV. Unlike U.S. GAAP, IFRS permits reversal of previous write-downs (up to the original cost) if NRV subsequently recovers. LIFO is also prohibited under IFRS.

Q58. Which of the following would be classified as an investing activity?

A) Payment of interest on notes payable
B) Collection of principal on a note receivable
C) Proceeds from issuing common stock
D) Payment of accounts payable

Answer: B) Collection of principal on a note receivable

Explanation: Investing activities relate to the acquisition and disposal of long-term assets and investments. Collecting principal on a note receivable is an investing cash inflow. Interest received can be operating or investing (GAAP = operating; IFRS allows either).

Q59. A company uses the aging method to estimate bad debts. The analysis shows required allowance of $45,000. The current allowance balance is $10,000 (credit). The bad debt expense for the period is:

A) $45,000
B) $35,000
C) $55,000
D) $10,000

Answer: B) $35,000

Explanation: Under the aging (balance sheet) method, the goal is to bring the allowance to the desired balance. Required allowance = $45,000; Existing balance = $10,000. Adjusting entry = $45,000 − $10,000 = $35,000 debit to Bad Debt Expense, credit to Allowance.

Q60. Which of the following is not a component of the statement of stockholders' equity?

A) Common stock
B) Additional paid-in capital
C) Treasury stock
D) Accounts payable

Answer: D) Accounts payable

Explanation: The statement of stockholders' equity includes components of equity: common stock, APIC, retained earnings, treasury stock, and accumulated OCI. Accounts payable is a liability and does not appear in stockholders' equity.

Q61. GASB standards apply to:

A) Publicly traded corporations
B) Private companies
C) State and local governmental entities
D) Not-for-profit hospitals only

Answer: C) State and local governmental entities

Explanation: GASB (Governmental Accounting Standards Board) establishes accounting and financial reporting standards for state and local governments in the U.S. FASB standards apply to private sector entities (corporations, non-profits). Federal agencies follow standards set by the FASAB.

Q62. In a not-for-profit organization, donor-restricted contributions are reported as:

A) Unrestricted net assets
B) Net assets with donor restrictions
C) Revenue only when spent
D) A liability until restrictions are met

Answer: B) Net assets with donor restrictions

Explanation: ASU 2016-14 simplified NFP net asset classification to two categories: (1) net assets without donor restrictions, and (2) net assets with donor restrictions. Restricted contributions are recognized as revenue when received and reported in the restricted category.

Q63. Under the treasury stock method, diluted EPS includes the dilutive effect of:

A) Antidilutive securities
B) Stock options, warrants, and convertible securities that would decrease EPS
C) Only convertible bonds
D) All outstanding options regardless of whether they increase or decrease EPS

Answer: B) Stock options, warrants, and convertible securities that would decrease EPS

Explanation: Diluted EPS includes only dilutive securities — those whose inclusion would decrease EPS (or increase loss per share). Antidilutive securities (those that would increase EPS) are excluded. Each security is tested individually before including in the computation.

Q64. The installment sales method recognizes gross profit:

A) At the point of sale
B) As cash is collected, based on gross profit percentage
C) When the final payment is received
D) On the accrual basis

Answer: B) As cash is collected, based on gross profit percentage

Explanation: Under the installment method (used in limited circumstances where collection is highly uncertain), gross profit is deferred and recognized proportionally as cash is collected: GP recognized = Cash collected × GP%. This method is rarely used under ASC 606.

Q65. Which of the following events requires a note disclosure but no adjustment to the financial statements?

A) Evidence of a customer's insolvency existing before year-end
B) Loss from a natural disaster after year-end
C) Settlement of a pre-existing lawsuit after year-end at the estimated amount
D) Discovery that inventory was overstated at year-end

Answer: B) Loss from a natural disaster after year-end

Explanation: A natural disaster occurring after the balance sheet date is a Type II subsequent event — a non-recognized event that requires disclosure only (no adjustment), since the condition did not exist at year-end. Types I events (conditions existing at year-end) require adjustment.

Q66. A company sells goods on a bill-and-hold arrangement. Revenue is recognized when:

A) The customer is billed
B) The customer pays
C) Control transfers to the customer
D) The goods are shipped

Answer: C) Control transfers to the customer

Explanation: Under ASC 606, revenue from bill-and-hold arrangements is recognized when control transfers. Control is transferred when the arrangement is substantive, the product is identified as belonging to the customer, the product is ready for physical transfer, and the seller lacks the ability to use it or direct it to another customer.

Q67. Which of the following is a contra-asset account?

A) Unearned revenue
B) Accumulated depreciation
C) Allowance for doubtful accounts
D) Both B and C

Answer: D) Both B and C

Explanation: Contra-asset accounts reduce the gross amount of an asset on the balance sheet. Accumulated depreciation offsets PP&E; Allowance for doubtful accounts offsets gross AR. Unearned revenue is a liability.

Q68. Impairment of a long-lived asset held for use under ASC 360 is recognized when:

A) Fair value drops below net book value
B) The carrying amount exceeds the sum of undiscounted future cash flows, and the loss equals the excess of carrying amount over fair value
C) The asset is abandoned
D) The asset's market value declines more than 25%

Answer: B) The carrying amount exceeds the sum of undiscounted future cash flows, and the loss equals the excess of carrying amount over fair value

Explanation: ASC 360 uses a two-step test: (1) Recoverability test — if carrying amount > undiscounted future cash flows, impairment exists; (2) Measurement — impairment loss = carrying amount − fair value. Once written down, the new carrying amount becomes the new cost basis (no reversal under GAAP).

Q69. A company had 200,000 shares outstanding on January 1. On July 1, it issued 60,000 new shares. Weighted-average shares for basic EPS equals:

A) 260,000
B) 230,000
C) 200,000
D) 260,000

Answer: B) 230,000

Explanation: Weighted-average = (200,000 × 6/12) + (260,000 × 6/12) = 100,000 + 130,000 = 230,000 shares. New shares are weighted by the fraction of the year they were outstanding.

Q70. Under IFRS, which inventory costing method is prohibited?

A) FIFO
B) Weighted-average
C) LIFO
D) Specific identification

Answer: C) LIFO

Explanation: IAS 2 prohibits the use of LIFO because it does not provide a reliable approximation of actual cost flows and can result in significantly understated inventory values during periods of rising prices. FIFO, weighted-average, and specific identification are all permitted under IFRS.

PART 2: Auditing and Attestation (AUD) — Questions 71–130

Q71. The primary purpose of an audit of financial statements is to:

A) Detect all fraud
B) Express an opinion on the fairness of the financial statements
C) Prepare the financial statements
D) Ensure the company's solvency

Answer: B) Express an opinion on the fairness of the financial statements

Explanation: An audit provides reasonable (not absolute) assurance that financial statements are free of material misstatement. The auditor expresses an opinion on whether statements are presented fairly in all material respects. Auditors are not guarantors against fraud, nor do they prepare statements.

Q72. Audit risk is the product of:

A) Inherent risk × Control risk
B) Inherent risk × Detection risk
C) Inherent risk × Control risk × Detection risk
D) Control risk × Detection risk

Answer: C) Inherent risk × Control risk × Detection risk

Explanation: Audit Risk (AR) = Inherent Risk (IR) × Control Risk (CR) × Detection Risk (DR). Inherent risk = susceptibility of assertion to material misstatement absent controls. Control risk = risk that controls won't prevent/detect misstatement. Detection risk = risk that auditor procedures won't detect remaining misstatement.

Q73. When an auditor assesses control risk as low, the auditor will:

A) Increase substantive testing
B) Reduce substantive testing
C) Eliminate substantive testing entirely
D) Increase inherent risk assessment

Answer: B) Reduce substantive testing

Explanation: Detection risk is inversely related to inherent and control risk. If control risk is assessed low (controls are effective), the auditor can accept higher detection risk, meaning less extensive substantive procedures are needed to achieve the same overall audit risk level.

Q74. Which type of audit opinion is issued when financial statements are materially misstated and the misstatement is pervasive?

A) Qualified opinion
B) Disclaimer of opinion
C) Adverse opinion
D) Unmodified opinion

Answer: C) Adverse opinion

Explanation: An adverse opinion is issued when misstatements are material AND pervasive — affecting many elements or fundamental to users' understanding. A qualified opinion ("except for") is used when the misstatement is material but not pervasive. A disclaimer is issued when unable to obtain sufficient evidence.

Q75. The concept of professional skepticism requires auditors to:

A) Assume management is dishonest
B) Accept all management representations without question
C) Maintain a questioning mind and critically assess evidence
D) Trust internal controls completely

Answer: C) Maintain a questioning mind and critically assess evidence

Explanation: Professional skepticism means the auditor neither assumes good faith nor assumes bad faith, but maintains a questioning attitude, critically evaluates evidence, and remains alert to conditions indicating possible misstatement. It does not mean distrust, but careful professional judgment.

Q76. Which of the following is not an auditing standard-setting body for public company audits?

A) PCAOB
B) SEC
C) AICPA
D) Both B and C

Answer: C) AICPA

Explanation: The PCAOB (Public Company Accounting Oversight Board) sets auditing standards for audits of SEC registrants (public companies). The SEC has oversight authority over PCAOB. The AICPA sets Statements on Auditing Standards (SAS) for audits of private companies, not public company audits.

Q77. Analytical procedures are required during which phase(s) of an audit?

A) Planning only
B) Completion (final review) only
C) Planning and completion
D) Planning, fieldwork, and completion

Answer: C) Planning and completion

Explanation: Under AU-C 520, analytical procedures are required during planning (to understand the entity and identify risk areas) and during the final review stage (to assess conclusions reached). During fieldwork, they may be used as substantive procedures but are not required.

Q78. An auditor discovers that a client has an immaterial error in the financial statements. The client refuses to correct it. The auditor should:

A) Issue an adverse opinion
B) Withdraw from the engagement
C) Issue an unmodified opinion
D) Issue a qualified opinion

Answer: C) Issue an unmodified opinion

Explanation: Because the error is immaterial, the financial statements are not materially misstated. An unmodified (clean) opinion remains appropriate. The auditor should document the uncorrected misstatement and consider it along with other accumulated misstatements in assessing materiality.

Q79. The auditor's responsibility for fraud is to:

A) Guarantee that all fraud is detected
B) Design procedures to provide reasonable assurance of detecting material fraud
C) Detect all misappropriation of assets
D) Report all fraud to law enforcement

Answer: B) Design procedures to provide reasonable assurance of detecting material fraud

Explanation: AU-C 240 requires auditors to assess risks of material misstatement due to fraud (fraudulent financial reporting and misappropriation of assets) and respond with appropriate procedures. Auditors provide reasonable, not absolute, assurance and are not required to detect all fraud.

Q80. Which assertion is most at risk when auditing accounts payable?

A) Existence
B) Completeness
C) Valuation
D) Rights and obligations

Answer: B) Completeness

Explanation: For liabilities (AP), the primary risk is understatement — that payables exist but aren't recorded. The completeness assertion asks: are all obligations that should be recorded actually included? Auditors test completeness by searching for unrecorded liabilities (reviewing subsequent disbursements, vendor statements).

Q81. An engagement letter serves to:

A) Replace the management representation letter
B) Document the audit plan
C) Define the terms of the engagement between auditor and client
D) Provide an audit opinion

Answer: C) Define the terms of the engagement between auditor and client

Explanation: The engagement letter is a contract between the auditor and client that clarifies: the objective and scope of the audit, the auditor's responsibilities, management's responsibilities, limitations of the audit, and the basis for fees. It is signed before the engagement begins.

Q82. Confirmation of accounts receivable is used primarily to test the assertion of:

A) Completeness
B) Valuation
C) Existence
D) Presentation and disclosure

Answer: C) Existence

Explanation: AR confirmation asks the debtor to verify that the balance exists and is owed. This primarily tests the existence assertion (recorded AR actually exists). Completeness would require searching for unrecorded receivables, which confirmation does not address effectively.

Q83. A qualified opinion due to a scope limitation uses which key phrase?

A) "In our opinion..."
B) "Except for the effects of..."
C) "Because of the significance of the matter..."
D) "The financial statements do not present fairly..."

Answer: B) "Except for the effects of..."

Explanation: A qualified opinion (either for a GAAP departure or scope limitation) uses "except for" language — the auditor is saying the opinion is clean except for the identified matter. An adverse opinion uses language that statements "do not present fairly." A disclaimer uses language about being "unable to express an opinion."

Q84. Independence for CPAs in public practice is required under:

A) The AICPA Code of Professional Conduct for all engagements
B) The AICPA Code for attestation and audit engagements
C) Tax engagements only
D) All client relationships

Answer: B) The AICPA Code for attestation and audit engagements

Explanation: Independence is required for attest engagements (audits, reviews, compilations when independence is not impaired, examinations). It is not required for tax, consulting, or bookkeeping services, though objectivity and integrity are always required.

Q85. The risk of material misstatement (RMM) is the combination of:

A) Control risk and detection risk
B) Inherent risk and control risk
C) Inherent risk and detection risk
D) Sampling risk and non-sampling risk

Answer: B) Inherent risk and control risk

Explanation: RMM = IR × CR. It represents the risk that a material misstatement exists in the financial statements before the auditor's procedures. The auditor cannot control IR or CR directly but responds to assessed RMM by adjusting detection risk (the nature, timing, and extent of substantive procedures).

Q86. Which of the following best describes a material weakness in internal control?

A) A control deficiency that could result in a material misstatement
B) A deficiency that is less severe than a significant deficiency
C) A minor control gap with no potential for misstatement
D) A control deficiency identified by management

Answer: A) A control deficiency that could result in a material misstatement

Explanation: A material weakness is a deficiency (or combination of deficiencies) in internal control over financial reporting such that there is a reasonable possibility that a material misstatement will not be prevented or detected on a timely basis. It is the most severe type — more serious than a significant deficiency.

Q87. The auditor's consideration of the going concern assumption requires:

A) Issuing an adverse opinion whenever doubt exists
B) Evaluating whether conditions raise substantial doubt and, if so, assessing management's plans
C) Guaranteeing the entity's continued existence
D) Adding an explanatory paragraph for all going concern matters

Answer: B) Evaluating whether conditions raise substantial doubt and, if so, assessing management's plans

Explanation: AU-C 570 requires auditors to evaluate conditions that may raise substantial doubt about an entity's ability to continue as a going concern for one year after the financial statement date. If doubt exists, management's mitigation plans are assessed. An explanatory paragraph is added to an otherwise unmodified opinion; a disclaimer may be appropriate in extreme cases.

Q88. Substantive procedures include:

A) Tests of controls and analytical procedures
B) Tests of details and substantive analytical procedures
C) Observation and inquiry only
D) Risk assessment procedures

Answer: B) Tests of details and substantive analytical procedures

Explanation: Substantive procedures are audit procedures designed to detect material misstatements. They consist of: (1) tests of details (examining transactions, balances, disclosures) and (2) substantive analytical procedures (comparing recorded amounts with expectations). Tests of controls are separate — they test the effectiveness of controls.

Q89. A compilation engagement provides:

A) Reasonable assurance on financial statements
B) Limited assurance
C) No assurance
D) Absolute assurance

Answer: C) No assurance

Explanation: In a compilation, the accountant assists in presenting financial information in the form of financial statements without undertaking to verify that information. No assurance is expressed. A review provides limited (negative) assurance. An audit provides reasonable (positive) assurance.

Q90. The AICPA's Code of Professional Conduct requires CPAs to maintain confidentiality of client information. Exceptions where disclosure is permitted without client consent include:

A) When a subpoena or court order is issued
B) A peer review of the CPA's practice
C) A complaint filed against the CPA
D) All of the above

Answer: D) All of the above

Explanation: The confidentiality rule (ET 1.700.001) permits disclosure without client consent in response to a valid subpoena, during peer review proceedings, and in response to AICPA or state board disciplinary proceedings. All three are recognized exceptions to the confidentiality requirement.

Q91. Statistical sampling allows the auditor to:

A) Eliminate sampling risk
B) Quantify and control sampling risk
C) Reduce non-sampling risk to zero
D) Avoid judgment in sample selection

Answer: B) Quantify and control sampling risk

Explanation: Statistical sampling uses probability-based selection and mathematical tools to measure sampling risk — the risk that the sample is not representative of the population. Non-statistical sampling relies on judgment and cannot quantify sampling risk. Neither method eliminates risk entirely.

Q92. When an auditor uses a specialist's work, the auditor:

A) May reference the specialist in the audit report
B) Is relieved of responsibility for the work
C) Must evaluate the specialist's competence and the reasonableness of findings
D) Must obtain the specialist's signature on the audit report

Answer: C) Must evaluate the specialist's competence and the reasonableness of findings

Explanation: AU-C 620 requires auditors to evaluate whether the specialist has the necessary competence, capabilities, and objectivity, and assess whether the specialist's findings are reasonable in the context of the audit. The auditor remains solely responsible for the opinion and does not reference the specialist in an unmodified report.

Q93. In an audit of internal controls over financial reporting (ICFR) for a public company under AS 2201 (PCAOB), the auditor:

A) Issues a separate opinion on ICFR
B) Issues an integrated opinion covering both financial statements and ICFR
C) Only tests controls, not the financial statements
D) Relies entirely on management's assessment

Answer: B) Issues an integrated opinion covering both financial statements and ICFR

Explanation: Under PCAOB AS 2201 (applicable to accelerated filers under SOX Section 404(b)), the audit of ICFR is integrated with the financial statement audit. The auditor issues an opinion on both the financial statements and management's assessment of ICFR (and may issue separate opinions on each).

Q94. A review of interim financial statements provides:

A) Positive assurance
B) Reasonable assurance
C) Limited (negative) assurance
D) No assurance

Answer: C) Limited (negative) assurance

Explanation: A review provides limited assurance — expressed in the negative form: "We are not aware of any material modifications that should be made…" This is less assurance than an audit (reasonable/positive assurance) and more than a compilation (no assurance). Reviews use primarily analytical procedures and inquiry.

Q95. Which of the following is the best evidence for confirming the existence of fixed assets?

A) Management inquiry
B) Physical inspection of the assets
C) Review of the depreciation schedule
D) Inspection of purchase invoices

Answer: B) Physical inspection of the assets

Explanation: Physical inspection (observation) provides direct evidence that the asset actually exists. While purchase invoices support valuation and rights, they do not confirm current existence. Inquiry is the weakest form of evidence. Physical observation is the most persuasive for the existence assertion.

Q96. The auditor's responsibility for detecting illegal acts is to:

A) Design procedures specifically to detect all illegal acts
B) Be aware that illegal acts could affect the financial statements and consider their effect
C) Report all suspected illegal acts to regulatory authorities
D) Withdraw from the engagement if any illegal acts are found

Answer: B) Be aware that illegal acts could affect the financial statements and consider their effect

Explanation: AU-C 250 distinguishes between illegal acts directly related to financial statement amounts (where auditors have responsibility similar to fraud) and those with an indirect effect. Auditors are not responsible for detecting all illegal acts but must consider their potential impact on the financial statements.

Q97. In attribute sampling used for tests of controls, a larger tolerable deviation rate results in:

A) A larger required sample size
B) A smaller required sample size
C) No change in sample size
D) A higher confidence level

Answer: B) A smaller required sample size

Explanation: Tolerable deviation rate (TDR) is the maximum rate of control failures the auditor is willing to accept. A higher TDR means the auditor is more tolerant of deviations, so fewer samples are needed to conclude whether the actual rate exceeds TDR. Conversely, a lower TDR requires larger samples.

Q98. Related party transactions require:

A) Disclosure in the notes to the financial statements
B) Elimination from the financial statements
C) No special treatment if at arm's length
D) Automatic qualification of the audit opinion

Answer: A) Disclosure in the notes to the financial statements

Explanation: ASC 850 and AU-C 550 require disclosure of related party transactions because users need this information to evaluate potential conflicts of interest and non-arm's-length terms. Related party transactions are not automatically eliminated (only in consolidation) and don't require qualification unless undisclosed or misstated.

Q99. Which type of audit report modification is used when the auditor cannot obtain sufficient appropriate evidence due to a client-imposed scope limitation?

A) Adverse opinion
B) Qualified opinion or disclaimer, depending on pervasiveness
C) Unmodified with emphasis-of-matter paragraph
D) Compilation report

Answer: B) Qualified opinion or disclaimer, depending on pervasiveness

Explanation: A client-imposed scope limitation (e.g., client won't allow AR confirmation) may result in a qualified opinion ("except for") if the matter is material but not pervasive, or a disclaimer of opinion if the matter is so pervasive the auditor cannot form any opinion. Client-imposed limitations are more serious than circumstance-imposed limitations.

Q100. The auditor's documentation (work papers) must be retained for how long after the report release date for audits under PCAOB standards?

A) 3 years
B) 5 years
C) 7 years
D) 10 years

Answer: C) 7 years

Explanation: PCAOB standards require audit documentation to be retained for at least 7 years from the report release date. The AICPA (for non-public company audits) requires 5 years. SOX established the 7-year requirement for public company audit records.

Q101. Under SAS standards, which of the following best describes negative confirmation?

A) The debtor is asked to respond only if they disagree with the stated balance
B) The debtor is asked to confirm the stated balance
C) The debtor is asked to provide their own balance
D) The auditor mails a statement to a non-existent address

Answer: A) The debtor is asked to respond only if they disagree with the stated balance

Explanation: Negative confirmations request a response only if the recipient disagrees with the information presented. They provide less evidence than positive confirmations because non-responses don't confirm agreement — recipients may simply ignore the request. They are appropriate when risk is low, accounts are numerous and small, and recipients are expected to respond if incorrect.

Q102. Which of the following procedures would not be performed during the planning phase?

A) Understanding the entity and its environment
B) Performing substantive tests of account balances
C) Developing the overall audit strategy
D) Assessing inherent risks

Answer: B) Performing substantive tests of account balances

Explanation: Substantive testing (testing transactions and balances) is performed during the fieldwork/execution phase, not planning. Planning involves: understanding the entity, assessing risks, establishing materiality, developing the audit strategy, and preparing the audit plan.

Q103. The three requirements of auditor independence under the AICPA Code are:

A) Competence, objectivity, confidentiality
B) Independence in fact, independence in appearance, and professional skepticism
C) Financial independence, mental independence, ethical independence
D) No financial interest, no family relationships, no consulting relationships

Answer: B) Independence in fact, independence in appearance, and professional skepticism

Explanation: Independence in fact means the auditor is actually unbiased. Independence in appearance means a reasonable observer would conclude the auditor is unbiased. Both are required. Professional skepticism is a related concept — a questioning mindset that supports independence.

Q104. A CPA firm's system of quality control includes all of the following except:

A) Engagement performance
B) Human resources
C) Client billing policies
D) Monitoring

Answer: C) Client billing policies

Explanation: SQCS 8 (now updated by QM standards) identifies quality control elements: (1) leadership responsibilities, (2) relevant ethical requirements, (3) acceptance and continuance, (4) human resources, (5) engagement performance, and (6) monitoring. Billing policies are an administrative/business matter, not a quality control element.

Q105. If an auditor concludes that there is substantial doubt about an entity's going concern, and management's plans adequately mitigate the doubt, the auditor should:

A) Issue an adverse opinion
B) Issue an unmodified opinion with an explanatory/emphasis-of-matter paragraph
C) Issue a disclaimer of opinion
D) Withdraw from the engagement

Answer: B) Issue an unmodified opinion with an explanatory/emphasis-of-matter paragraph

Explanation: If management's plans adequately mitigate the going concern doubt, the auditor includes an explanatory paragraph (emphasis-of-matter) in an otherwise unmodified report, drawing attention to the uncertainty. If doubt is NOT mitigated, the auditor may still issue a modified or unmodified opinion with the paragraph, and in extreme cases a disclaimer.

Q106. The auditor's assessment of materiality is used to:

A) Guarantee detection of all misstatements above materiality
B) Determine the nature, timing, and extent of audit procedures
C) Establish the maximum error clients may make
D) Calculate the exact size of audit samples

Answer: B) Determine the nature, timing, and extent of audit procedures

Explanation: Materiality guides audit planning and evaluation. Higher materiality = less extensive testing needed; lower materiality = more extensive. It is a matter of professional judgment — not a formula. The auditor aims to detect misstatements that, individually or in aggregate, could influence economic decisions of users.

Q107. A CPA discovers during an audit that the client has paid fictitious invoices to a shell company controlled by a manager. This is an example of:

A) Fraudulent financial reporting
B) Misappropriation of assets
C) A material weakness in ICFR
D) An illegal act

Answer: B) Misappropriation of assets

Explanation: Misappropriation of assets (employee fraud/theft) involves stealing or misusing company assets. Paying fictitious invoices to divert funds is a classic misappropriation scheme. Fraudulent financial reporting involves intentional misstatement of financial results to deceive investors/creditors.

Q108. In performing a review engagement for a nonpublic entity, an accountant must:

A) Test internal controls
B) Obtain a complete understanding of internal controls
C) Apply analytical procedures and make inquiries
D) Issue a written communication providing positive assurance

Answer: C) Apply analytical procedures and make inquiries

Explanation: A review under SSARS consists primarily of analytical procedures applied to financial data and inquiries of management. It does not require testing internal controls or obtaining an understanding of ICFR. The report provides limited (negative) assurance, not positive assurance.

Q109. The concept of audit evidence requires that evidence be:

A) Always obtained from external sources
B) Sufficient and appropriate
C) Documented in bound form
D) Prepared by the client

Answer: B) Sufficient and appropriate

Explanation: Audit evidence must be sufficient (quantity — enough to support the opinion) and appropriate (quality — relevant and reliable). External evidence is generally more reliable than internal, but the form of evidence matters less than these two criteria.

Q110. Under SAS, when an auditor uses the work of internal auditors, the external auditor:

A) Assumes full responsibility for their work
B) Must evaluate their competence and objectivity before relying on their work
C) Can eliminate substantive testing entirely
D) Is prohibited from relying on internal audit work

Answer: B) Must evaluate their competence and objectivity before relying on their work

Explanation: AU-C 610 permits external auditors to use the work of internal auditors after evaluating their competence, objectivity, and the application of systematic and disciplined approaches. The external auditor remains solely responsible for the audit opinion and cannot delegate that responsibility.

PART 3: Regulation (REG) — Questions 111–170

Q111. For federal income tax purposes, gross income includes:

Answer: D) Wages and salaries

Explanation: IRC §61 defines gross income as all income from whatever source derived. Wages are always included. Gifts (§102), inheritances (§102), and child support (§71) are specifically excluded from gross income.

Q112. A self-employed individual may deduct health insurance premiums:

Explanation: IRC §162(l) allows self-employed individuals to deduct 100% of health insurance premiums paid for themselves, spouses, and dependents as an adjustment to income (above-the-line deduction), reducing AGI. The deduction cannot exceed net self-employment income.

Q113. The standard deduction for a married filing jointly couple in 2024 is approximately:

A) $13,850
B) $20,800
C) $29,200
D) $27,700

Answer: C) $29,200

Explanation: For 2024, the standard deduction for Married Filing Jointly is $29,200 (up from $27,700 in 2023). Single filers get $14,600; Head of Household gets $21,900. These amounts are adjusted annually for inflation.

Q114. A C corporation's dividends received deduction (DRD) is 70% when:

B) The recipient owns 20% or more but less than 80%
C) The recipient owns 80% or more
D) The corporation files a consolidated return

Answer: A) The recipient owns less than 20% of the distributing corporation

Explanation: The DRD rates: 50% (ownership < 20% — note: tax law updated this to 50% for 2018+), 65% (ownership 20%–80%), 100% (ownership 80%+ filing consolidated). Pre-TCJA rates were 70%/80%/100%. The current rates under TCJA are 50%/65%/100%.

Q115. For individual taxpayers, capital gains on assets held more than one year are taxed at:

A) Ordinary income rates
B) 0%, 15%, or 20% depending on taxable income
C) Always 15%
D) Always 20%

Answer: B) 0%, 15%, or 20% depending on taxable income

Explanation: Long-term capital gains (assets held > 12 months) are taxed at preferential rates: 0% for lower-income taxpayers, 15% for middle-income, and 20% for high-income taxpayers. The applicable rate depends on the taxpayer's taxable income relative to bracket thresholds.

Q116. Which of the following is a deductible business expense?

A) Federal income taxes
B) Penalties for violating federal law
C) Ordinary and necessary business expenses
D) Political contributions

Answer: C) Ordinary and necessary business expenses

Explanation: IRC §162 allows deduction of ordinary and necessary expenses paid in carrying on a trade or business. Federal income taxes, fines/penalties for violating federal law (§162(f)), and political contributions are specifically non-deductible.

Q117. A taxpayer sells stock for $15,000 that was purchased 3 years ago for $20,000. The $5,000 loss is:

A) Deductible in full as an ordinary loss
B) A capital loss, deductible up to $3,000 against ordinary income per year
C) Not deductible
D) Deductible only against capital gains

Answer: B) A capital loss, deductible up to $3,000 against ordinary income per year

Explanation: Stock held as a capital asset held more than one year generates a long-term capital loss. Capital losses first offset capital gains; excess capital losses can offset up to $3,000 of ordinary income per year ($1,500 MFS). Unused losses carry forward indefinitely.

Q118. The alternative minimum tax (AMT) for individuals applies when:

A) The regular tax is less than the tentative minimum tax
B) Gross income exceeds $500,000

D) Capital gains exceed $50,000

Answer: A) The regular tax is less than the tentative minimum tax

Explanation: AMT liability = Tentative Minimum Tax (TMT) − Regular Tax (if positive). TMT is calculated on Alternative Minimum Taxable Income (AMTI) at a 26%/28% rate, after applying the AMT exemption. Post-TCJA, AMT exemptions and phase-out thresholds increased significantly, shielding most middle-income taxpayers.

Q119. A sole proprietor reports business income and losses on:

B) Form 1120
C) Form 1065
D) Schedule E of Form 1040

Answer: A) Schedule C of Form 1040

Explanation: Schedule C (Profit or Loss from Business) is used by sole proprietors to report business revenue and expenses. Q120. Under the AICPA's Statements on Standards for Tax Services (SSTS), a CPA may take a tax return position that is:

Explanation: Quick ratio = (Current assets − Inventory) / Current liabilities = ($300,000 − $80,000) / $150,000 = $220,000 / $150,000 = 1.47. This indicates the company has $1.47 of liquid assets for every $1.00 of current liabilities.

Q174. Horizon (terminal) value in a DCF model represents:

A) The value of projected free cash flows during the forecast period
B) The value of all cash flows beyond the explicit forecast period
C) The current market price of the company
D) The liquidation value of assets

Answer: B) The value of all cash flows beyond the explicit forecast period

Explanation: Terminal value captures the value of a business beyond the detailed forecast period (typically 5–10 years). It is usually calculated as a growing perpetuity: TV = FCF(n+1) / (WACC − g), or using an exit multiple. Terminal value often represents 60–80% of total enterprise value in DCF analyses.

Q175. The weighted average cost of capital (WACC) is used to:

A) Value debt securities only
B) Discount free cash flows to determine enterprise value
C) Measure profitability
D) Determine optimal inventory levels

Answer: B) Discount free cash flows to determine enterprise value

Explanation: WACC represents the blended required return across all capital providers (debt and equity), weighted by their proportions. It is used as the discount rate in DCF valuation (for unlevered free cash flows) and in capital budgeting (as the hurdle rate). WACC = (E/V × Re) + (D/V × Rd × (1−T)).

Q176. Which of the following is not a method used to value a business?

A) Discounted cash flow (DCF)
B) Market comparables (comparable company analysis)
C) Precedent transactions
D) Payback period

Answer: D) Payback period

Explanation: Business valuation methods include DCF (intrinsic value), comparable company analysis (market multiples), precedent transactions (acquisition multiples), and asset-based approaches. The payback period is a capital budgeting tool that measures how quickly an investment recovers its cost — not a business valuation method.

Q177. In managerial accounting, contribution margin is calculated as:

A) Revenue minus total costs
B) Revenue minus variable costs
C) Gross profit minus operating expenses
D) Net income plus fixed costs

Answer: B) Revenue minus variable costs

Explanation: Contribution Margin (CM) = Revenue − Variable Costs. CM measures what revenue contributes to covering fixed costs and profit. CM per unit = Selling price − Variable cost per unit. It is central to CVP (cost-volume-profit) analysis, breakeven calculations, and pricing decisions.

Q178. The breakeven point in units is calculated as:

A) Fixed costs / Contribution margin ratio
B) Fixed costs / Contribution margin per unit
C) Total costs / Revenue
D) Variable costs / Selling price

Answer: B) Fixed costs / Contribution margin per unit

Explanation: Breakeven units = Fixed Costs / (Selling Price − Variable Cost per unit) = Fixed Costs / CM per unit. At this point, total revenue = total costs and profit = $0. In sales dollars: BEP$ = Fixed Costs / CM Ratio = Fixed Costs / (CM/Revenue).

Q179. A flexible budget:

A) Is fixed at the beginning of the year
B) Adjusts for actual activity levels
C) Is the same as a static budget
D) Only applies to manufacturing companies

Answer: B) Adjusts for actual activity levels

Explanation: A flexible budget adjusts variable costs and revenues for the actual level of activity achieved, making variances more meaningful. Unlike a static budget (set at one volume level), a flexible budget "flexes" to show what costs should have been at actual volume — enabling accurate performance evaluation.

Q180. Standard costing uses direct labor efficiency variance to measure:

A) The difference between actual and standard wage rates
B) The difference between actual hours worked and standard hours allowed, at the standard rate
C) The difference between actual and budgeted overhead
D) The total labor cost variance

Answer: B) The difference between actual hours worked and standard hours allowed, at the standard rate

Explanation: Direct Labor Efficiency Variance = (Actual Hours − Standard Hours Allowed) × Standard Rate. A favorable variance (actual hours < standard) indicates better-than-expected efficiency. The Direct Labor Rate Variance = (Actual Rate − Standard Rate) × Actual Hours.

Q181. Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) under the COSO framework identifies which primary categories of risk?

A) Financial, operational, strategic, compliance
B) Credit risk, market risk, liquidity risk
C) Fraud risk, error risk, regulatory risk
D) People risk, process risk, technology risk

Answer: A) Financial, operational, strategic, compliance

Explanation: The COSO ERM framework categorizes objectives (and related risks) into: Strategic, Operations, Reporting, and Compliance (SORC). Risks threaten the achievement of these objectives. The updated 2017 COSO ERM framework emphasizes linking risk management with strategy and performance.

Q182. Data analytics in auditing and accounting commonly uses which technique to identify unusual transactions?

A) Linear programming
B) Benford's Law analysis
C) LIFO allocation
D) Payback analysis

Answer: B) Benford's Law analysis

Explanation: Benford's Law predicts that in naturally occurring data, the leading digit is "1" about 30% of the time, "2" about 17%, etc. Transactions that deviate significantly from this distribution may indicate manipulation or fraud. Auditors use Benford's Law as an analytical procedure to flag unusual patterns in large datasets.

Q183. In a balanced scorecard, the learning and growth perspective focuses on:

A) Financial results and profitability
B) Customer satisfaction and retention
C) Internal process efficiency
D) Employee capabilities, information systems, and organizational culture

Answer: D) Employee capabilities, information systems, and organizational culture

Explanation: The balanced scorecard (Kaplan & Norton) has four perspectives: (1) Financial, (2) Customer, (3) Internal Business Processes, and (4) Learning and Growth. The learning and growth perspective addresses the infrastructure needed for the other perspectives — employee skills, technology, and culture to drive long-term improvement.

Q184. A company's debt-to-equity ratio is 2.0. This means:

A) The company has twice as much equity as debt
B) The company has twice as much debt as equity
C) The company is debt-free
D) The company's interest coverage ratio is 2.0

Answer: B) The company has twice as much debt as equity

Explanation: Debt-to-Equity = Total Debt / Total Equity = 2.0 means for every $1 of equity, there is $2 of debt. A higher D/E ratio indicates more financial leverage and risk. Lenders and analysts use this to assess solvency and capital structure.

Q185. Which of the following best describes the internal rate of return (IRR)?

A) The discount rate that makes NPV equal to zero
B) The accounting rate of return on investment
C) The cost of equity capital
D) The minimum acceptable rate of return

Answer: A) The discount rate that makes NPV equal to zero

Explanation: IRR is the rate that equates the present value of future cash flows to the initial investment (NPV = 0). A project is accepted if IRR > required return (WACC or hurdle rate). IRR can be misleading for mutually exclusive projects or non-conventional cash flows — NPV is generally preferred.

Q186. A company reports days sales outstanding (DSO) of 60 days. If the industry average is 35 days, this suggests:

A) The company collects receivables faster than the industry
B) The company has slower collections than the industry, indicating potential collection issues
C) The company is more profitable than competitors
D) Inventory is turning over too slowly

Answer: B) The company has slower collections than the industry, indicating potential collection issues

Explanation: DSO = (Accounts Receivable / Net Sales) × 365. A higher DSO means it takes longer to collect cash from customers. Compared to the 35-day industry average, 60 days signals possible collection problems, liberal credit policies, or customers in financial distress.

Q187. Which of the following is a key performance indicator (KPI) for operational efficiency in a manufacturing context?

A) Return on equity
B) Overall equipment effectiveness (OEE)
C) Debt service coverage ratio
D) Economic value added (EVA)

Answer: B) Overall equipment effectiveness (OEE)

Explanation: OEE measures manufacturing productivity by combining availability, performance, and quality rates. It is a pure operational KPI: OEE = Availability × Performance × Quality. Financial KPIs (ROE, DSCR, EVA) measure financial results, not direct operational efficiency of manufacturing processes.

Q188. Activity-based costing (ABC) differs from traditional costing by:

A) Using a single plant-wide overhead rate
B) Allocating overhead based on multiple cost drivers that reflect actual resource consumption
C) Using direct labor hours for all overhead allocation
D) Eliminating the need for cost allocation

Answer: B) Allocating overhead based on multiple cost drivers that reflect actual resource consumption

Explanation: ABC identifies activities that consume resources and assigns costs to products based on their actual consumption of those activities. Traditional costing uses one or two broad allocation bases (direct labor hours, machine hours), which can distort product costs. ABC provides more accurate cost information, especially when products differ significantly in complexity.

Q189. Economic Value Added (EVA) measures:

A) Net income after taxes
B) After-tax operating income minus the cost of capital employed
C) Market capitalization minus book value
D) Revenue growth rate

Answer: B) After-tax operating income minus the cost of capital employed

Explanation: EVA = NOPAT − (Capital × WACC), where NOPAT = Net Operating Profit After Tax. EVA is positive (value created) when operating returns exceed the cost of capital employed. It ties management compensation to true value creation, addressing weaknesses of accounting-based metrics.

Q190. In project management, the critical path is:

A) The shortest sequence of tasks in a project
B) The longest sequence of dependent tasks determining the minimum project duration
C) Tasks requiring the most resources
D) The sequence of tasks most likely to fail

Answer: B) The longest sequence of dependent tasks determining the minimum project duration

Explanation: The critical path is the longest path through a project network — the sequence of tasks where any delay extends the total project duration. Tasks on the critical path have zero float/slack. Project managers focus on critical path tasks to keep projects on schedule.

Q191. A company's inventory turnover ratio of 12 means:

A) The company holds 12 months of inventory
B) The company turns over (sells and replaces) its inventory 12 times per year
C) Inventory is growing 12% annually
D) Days in inventory is 12

Answer: B) The company turns over (sells and replaces) its inventory 12 times per year

Explanation: Inventory Turnover = COGS / Average Inventory. A ratio of 12 means inventory is fully replaced 12 times per year — approximately every 30 days (365/12). Higher turnover is generally favorable (efficient inventory management) but may indicate stockout risk. Days in inventory = 365 / Turnover = 30 days.

Q192. The concept of transfer pricing refers to:

A) Prices charged for international exports
B) Prices set for transactions between divisions or subsidiaries within the same company
C) Market prices for commodity trading
D) The cost of transferring employees between departments

Answer: B) Prices set for transactions between divisions or subsidiaries within the same company

Explanation: Transfer pricing establishes the internal prices at which goods, services, or intellectual property are exchanged between related entities. Tax authorities require arm's-length pricing for intercompany transactions to prevent tax base shifting to lower-tax jurisdictions. Internally, transfer prices affect division profitability and performance evaluation.

Q193. Regression analysis in financial modeling is used to:

A) Calculate exact future values with certainty
B) Identify relationships between variables and forecast outcomes based on historical data
C) Determine the present value of cash flows
D) Rank projects by profitability

Answer: B) Identify relationships between variables and forecast outcomes based on historical data

Explanation: Regression analysis (linear, multiple, etc.) examines statistical relationships between dependent and independent variables. In accounting/finance, it's used to: separate fixed and variable cost components, forecast revenues, identify cost drivers, and analyze trends. It provides probabilistic estimates, not certainties.

Q194. Which financial ratio measures a company's ability to meet long-term debt obligations from operating earnings?

A) Current ratio
B) Debt service coverage ratio (DSCR)
C) Quick ratio
D) Accounts payable turnover

Answer: B) Debt service coverage ratio (DSCR)

Explanation: DSCR = Net Operating Income / Total Debt Service (principal + interest payments). A DSCR above 1.0 means the company generates sufficient operating income to cover debt payments. Lenders commonly require a minimum DSCR of 1.25x. Current and quick ratios measure short-term liquidity, not long-term debt coverage.

Q195. A company considering outsourcing a component should include which of the following in the relevant cost analysis?

A) Sunk costs of previous equipment purchases
B) Avoidable fixed overhead that will be eliminated if production stops
C) Depreciation on existing equipment
D) Allocated corporate overhead

Answer: B) Avoidable fixed overhead that will be eliminated if production stops

Explanation: Relevant costs for make-or-buy decisions include only future, incremental costs that differ between alternatives: variable production costs, avoidable fixed costs, and opportunity costs. Sunk costs (past equipment), depreciation (non-cash allocation), and unavoidable allocated overhead are irrelevant — they don't change based on the decision.

Q196. The Sarbanes-Oxley Act Section 404(a) requires management to:

A) Certify quarterly earnings
B) Assess and report on the effectiveness of internal controls over financial reporting
C) Hire an internal audit function
D) Maintain a minimum number of independent directors

Answer: B) Assess and report on the effectiveness of internal controls over financial reporting

Explanation: SOX §404(a) requires management to assess the effectiveness of the company's ICFR as of year-end and include that assessment in the annual report (Form 10-K). §404(b) additionally requires the external auditor to attest to and report on management's ICFR assessment for accelerated filers.

Q197. A scenario analysis in financial forecasting:

A) Provides a single point estimate of future results
B) Evaluates outcomes under different sets of assumptions (optimistic, base, pessimistic)
C) Uses only historical data without assumptions
D) Is identical to sensitivity analysis

Answer: B) Evaluates outcomes under different sets of assumptions (optimistic, base, pessimistic)

Explanation: Scenario analysis models complete alternative futures (e.g., base case, bull case, bear case) by varying multiple inputs simultaneously. Sensitivity analysis changes one variable at a time to see its impact. Both help assess risk and uncertainty in projections, but scenario analysis captures interdependencies between variables.

Q198. The gross profit margin formula is:

A) Net income / Net revenue
B) (Net revenue − COGS) / Net revenue
C) Operating income / Net revenue
D) EBITDA / Net revenue

Answer: B) (Net revenue − COGS) / Net revenue

Explanation: Gross Profit Margin = Gross Profit / Net Revenue = (Revenue − COGS) / Revenue. It measures production/service efficiency before operating expenses. A declining gross margin may indicate rising input costs, pricing pressure, or product mix shifts. It differs from net margin (which includes all expenses) and operating margin (which includes operating expenses).

Q199. Under responsibility accounting, a profit center manager is responsible for:

A) Only costs
B) Only revenues
C) Both revenues and costs (but not invested capital)
D) Revenues, costs, and return on investment

Answer: C) Both revenues and costs (but not invested capital)

Explanation: Responsibility centers: Cost center (costs only), Revenue center (revenues only), Profit center (revenues and costs = profit), Investment center (revenues, costs, and capital = ROI/EVA). Profit center managers have P&L responsibility but typically don't control investment decisions, which are made at higher levels.

Q200. Which of the following best describes blockchain technology's impact on accounting?

A) It eliminates the need for financial statements
B) It provides an immutable, distributed ledger that enhances transaction transparency and may reduce reconciliation needs
C) It automates audit opinions
D) It replaces GAAP with technology-based standards

Answer: B) It provides an immutable, distributed ledger that enhances transaction transparency and may reduce reconciliation needs

Explanation: Blockchain creates a shared, tamper-resistant record of transactions that is updated in real time and verified across multiple nodes. For accounting, this means: near-real-time transaction recording, reduced reconciliation between counterparties, enhanced audit trails, and potential for continuous auditing.
